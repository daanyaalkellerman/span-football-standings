# AI Collaboration Reflection

For this assessment, I decided to use Java because it is a programming language I had already started learning and wanted to develop my skills in further. Although I have experience with other programming languages, I saw this as an opportunity to challenge myself and gain more practical experience with Java. I am also interested in expanding my career opportunities into areas such as quality assurance (QA), so working with Java, Maven and JUnit was particularly valuable to me.

I used OpenAI Codex throughout the assessment to assist with understanding the requirements, researching the historical football rules, developing the application and testing the solution. One thing I found particularly useful was learning how to structure my prompts more effectively. Instead of asking the AI to build the entire application at once, I broke the assessment into smaller stages, focusing on the requirements, historical research, implementation and testing separately. This made it easier to review the work and identify areas that needed further attention.

An important decision was making sure the application followed the correct football rules for the 1974/75 season rather than modern rules. Through the research process, I established that wins were worth two points and that goal average was used to rank teams with equal points. Another challenge was interpreting what week 10 meant. I chose to use the cumulative standings up to 28 September 1974 and document that assumption. Refining my prompts helped me get more detailed research, verify the historical match data and avoid rushing into the implementation without understanding the requirements.

During the production-readiness review, Codex also identified an issue where using a filesystem root as an output path caused a NullPointerException. The issue was reproduced, corrected and covered by additional automated tests. I then ran the Maven verification myself and confirmed that all 43 tests passed. This showed me why testing should include unusual inputs and error conditions, not only situations where the application works as expected.

Overall, this assessment helped me gain more practical exposure to Java development, Maven and JUnit testing. It also changed how I approach working with AI. I learned that the quality and clarity of my prompts have a direct impact on the usefulness of the responses, but that I still need to evaluate decisions, verify results and understand the code. I see AI as a useful development tool, especially for learning and problem-solving, but not as a replacement for my own judgement and responsibility as a developer.
